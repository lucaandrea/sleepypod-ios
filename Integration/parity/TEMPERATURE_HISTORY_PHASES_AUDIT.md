# Temperature history and Night/Dawn reconciliation

Source audit: 26 October 2026, Core `cc8d4d0f74102bbfb056000b825b0112ff06f45d`. This change remains part of the parity work; app version/build stays 0.2.0 (7). No physical Pod commands or release uploads were issued by these checks.

## Native behavior

- Existing Sleepypod temperature controls, three-tab navigation, cards and typography remain. A shared Bed history page adds the previous/current night, saved calendar targets, active Run Once, recorded power and actual presence intervals, with a read-only inspection slider. Tonight's Autopilot windows, manual holds and recorded decisions use the existing native timeline.
- History never presents planned targets as delivered telemetry. Date labels use the Pod timezone. Missing target buckets stay gaps; absent/malformed presence intervals never erase reported bed exits. Open sessions stop at the read time. Errors for thermal history, each side's sleep records and Run Once remain independent. The 24-hour power coverage and 100-record response limit are visible.
- Timing comparisons require reported presence and a difference of at least 15 minutes. They describe entry/exit relative to planned targets; they do not infer that an empty bed was actually heating. The chart states that saved schedules may have changed since a historical night.
- Stage adds recorded activity and current Run Once to its existing timeline. Run Once starts at the actual session start, later points use Core's Pod-clock today-or-tomorrow rule, and its expiry is exclusive. Preview withholds targets after a failed/stale Run Once read. Stage side/region controls stack at accessibility text sizes.
- Night/Dawn previously called `schedules.getNightPhases` and `schedules.setNightPhase`, which do not exist in the audited Core. Native now uses `GET /api/schedules?side=…&unit=F` and `POST /api/schedules/batch` through the existing owner connection.
- Phase grouping follows the web: before 04:00 belongs to the previous schedule day; enabled points with matching times, temperatures and power end action share edits. Dawn is the final quarter; means are weighted by held duration, including the final 30 minutes. A first edit on an unscheduled night creates the balanced template on unscheduled days, retaining alarms and using the configured end action.
- Both phase edits debounce into one batch per side. Source temperature/power rows and the owning day are rechecked before sending. This is read-before-write, **not server-side compare-and-swap**. A changed schedule, failed write, false acknowledgement or mismatched readback retains the pending adjustment and blocks automatic retry until explicit reload. Owner changes/backgrounding cancel unsent edits and discard old results.
- The shared preset algorithm now matches Core's JavaScript rounding (including negative halves), short-night bounds and redundant-point simplification. Warm-only ranges retain the web's neutral transition points; all saved points remain within hardware limits. Fifteen literal examples are generated from immutable Core source.

## Calendar distinction found during the audit

The web's `nightCurve()` shifts a saved night's morning points into the following day. The audited controller's `scheduleTemperature()` builds weekly cron directly from the row's day and clock. Native retains actual calendar-row semantics and explicit power `offDayOffset`; it does not silently move a Sunday's setpoint into Monday to imitate the web chart. The discrepancy was sent to the Core owner chat. Timing labels refer to current planned targets, not a claim that the historical schedule or a physical action was verified.
